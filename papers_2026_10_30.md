## 1. Uncertainty-aware Feedback Loop Diagnosis: integrating reinforcement learning and conformal prediction for reliable wafer defect classification

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Aleksandr Dekhovich, Carlas Smith, Oleg Soloviev
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40421v1

**Abstract**

Semiconductor manufacturing is a complex, multi-step process that requires both high precision and low operating costs. Therefore, detecting defects in a high-volume setting is an important component of wafer map analysis for reliable production. However, most state-of-the-art deep learning approaches require a full scan of the surface, which hinders the development of high-throughput inspection systems. To address this problem, we propose Uncertainty-aware Feedback Loop Diagnosis (UFLD), a novel algorithm that employs reinforcement learning for adaptive measurement acquisition and provides a prediction uncertainty level after each step, stopping the process once sufficient information is obtained. Unlike existing methods that rely on ensemble models or auxiliary confidence estimation deep learning models, UFLD utilizes a more parameter-efficient conformal prediction framework to determine the optimal stopping time. The proposed UFLD reduces acquisition time by about $40\%$ and achieves $98.4\%$ inspection accuracy, without requiring an additional learned confidence-estimation network. We validated our approach on the MixedWM38 dataset, demonstrating its advantages over existing adaptive acquisition procedures and standard convolutional neural networks.

## 2. TextSeal2: Distortion-Free LLM Watermarking with Copula Mixing and Memoryless Races

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Tom Sander, Pierre Fernandez, Itai Gat, Haiming Bao, Jiaxin Shi, Jason Park, Valeriu Lacatusu, Sylvestre-Alvise Rebuffi, Hongyan Chang, Hady Elsahar, Tomáš Souček, Tuan Tran, Surya Parimi, Alexandre Mourachko
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40613v1

**Abstract**

We present TextSeal2, a distortion-free watermark for large language models that keeps its signal under speculative decoding: tokens that replace rejected drafts remain watermarked with the same keys, and the acceptance rate is unchanged. It also preserves output diversity and adds no measurable inference latency. Its predecessor, TextSeal, restores diversity in the Gumbel-max scheme by routing each token to one of two keys via a coin flip. This is suboptimal because the detector averages the scores from both keys without knowing which key was used. TextSeal2 instead merges two or more keys into a single virtual key and couples it to fresh noise through AutoMark's Gaussian copula. Any number of keys, including publicly shared keys, can be merged this way without diluting detection. Under speculative decoding, TextSeal2 verifies keyed drafts with one more exponential race, run on the leftovers of the draft races. By memorylessness, this race reproduces block verification, so the output follows the target model exactly. We evaluate TextSeal2 on three LLMs. At equal output token diversity, TextSeal2 detects 8 to 22 percentage points more 100-token answers than TextSeal at a false-positive rate of $10^{-3}$, and is comparable to textGrain while requiring no post-logits optimization. Under speculative decoding, it detects 18 and 22 percentage points more 200-token answers than TextSeal and textGrain, respectively, at the same acceptance rate.

## 3. ShallowDefend: Harnessing Shallow Safety Alignment for Multi-Turn Defender

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Qiyao Ma, Xiaodong Liu, Weiwei Yang, Mingqian Feng, Chengquan Guo, Jialin Song, Carolyn Buractaon, Zhe Zhao, Junshan Zhang, Sandeep Atluri, Christopher White, Jianfeng Gao
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40627v1

**Abstract**

Safety alignment has enabled large language models to reliably refuse harmful requests directly, yet the same request distributed across a multi-turn conversation still succeeds far too often. This brittleness has a mechanistic root: Alignment is shallow, with safety behavior concentrated in a model's first few output tokens, and existing defenses inherit the weakness, supervising the surface form of the response without ever giving the model an explicit safety decision to learn. We take the opposite view of shallowness: if a short span determines the trajectory of an entire response, that span is the highest-leverage place to intervene, and the defender can occupy it first. We propose Assess $\to$ Decide $\to$ Respond, a framework in which the defender assesses the user's intent, commits to an explicit, parseable safety decision, and only then generates a reply conditioned on it, with the commitment trained by reinforcement learning at the payload turn. Because the decision is a token span, the reward can be a rule: a self-verified reward checks the committed label against the conversation's known nature, requiring no judge and no annotation, while the same format also supports an LLM-as-judge reward over a graded decision space with safe redirection. Across two model families and fourteen benchmarks spanning safety, utility, and helpfulness, our defenders achieve the strongest multi-turn safety in a controlled comparison, maintaining their lead in safety while preserving utility at a modest cost to helpfulness. A per-token KL analysis and interventional probes confirm why this works: training concentrates its effect on the brief deliberation that sets a response's trajectory, and the decision committed there is a causal control point. The very shallowness attackers exploit becomes the mechanism of defense.

## 4. Self-training for cross-centre coronary vessel segmentation in X-ray angiography using pre-contrast frame subtraction

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Danial Moafi, Aida Moafi, Mostafa Aryafar, Ashkan Sami, Maryam Aryafar
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40302v1

**Abstract**

Deep learning models for coronary vessel segmentation in X-ray angiography often perform poorly on data from centres that were not represented in training. Apart from differences in equipment, the labels of the largest public dataset (ARCADE) are incomplete, as only the segments defined in the SYNTAX score are annotated. In this study, we evaluated whether self-training with pseudo-labels can improve performance on external data without additional manual annotation. Pseudo-labels were generated from two sources, namely the predictions of an nnU-Net model trained on ARCADE and a contrast map obtained by subtracting a pre-contrast frame from the same sequence. The nnU-Net predictions were extended along the contrast map, and pixels detected only in the subtraction image were assigned an ignore label and excluded from the loss. Six models were trained and tested on ARCADE and on two external datasets (XCAD and XACV). All first-round models initialised with the baseline predictions performed better than both the baseline and a control model fine-tuned for the same number of epochs. The model trained on the XCAD pool improved the macro-averaged Dice score by 2.7 points (95% CI 2.3-3.2), while the control decreased it by 0.6 points. A second round of self-training did not lead to further improvement. When an external model trained on 50 fully annotated images was used to generate the initial labels, the XCAD Dice score increased from 0.623 to 0.745 without any XCAD labels, although performance on XACV did not improve because large main vessels were missed. These results suggest that the benefit of self-training depends largely on the sensitivity of the model used to generate the initial pseudo-labels.

## 5. PhysCount: Do MLLMs Count Visual Appearances or Physical Entities?

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Lidong Lu, Jiaxin Lv, Liangrui Dong, Xinyi Mao, Lidong Zou, Zhenquan Li, Tong Lu, Guo Chen
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40530v1

**Abstract**

Images reveal appearances, but counting often asks about physical objects. A single entity may be visible directly, in several reflections, or through glass, so detecting every visible occurrence does not determine how many entities are present. Existing visual counting benchmarks rarely separate these two operations. We introduce PhysCount, a benchmark for evaluating whether multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can distinguish what appears in an image from what physically exists in the scene. PhysCount contains 546 real-world images and 4,595 human-written questions organized into 651 target-specific groups. For each group, annotators locate the relevant appearances, label how each is observed, and identify appearances of the same entity. Four complementary question families test the enumeration of appearances, the enumeration of physical entities, image-formation-specific counting, and appearance--entity correspondence. We measure both accuracy on each family and the proportion of groups answered entirely correctly. Across 40 MLLMs, counting visible appearances is substantially easier than recovering the identities that connect them. Even the strongest model answers every question correctly in only 13.36\% of groups. Performance declines when one entity produces many appearances or when its appearances cross observation modes. Higher image resolution improves access to visual evidence but brings limited gains in complete-group accuracy, whereas answering related questions jointly produces a marked improvement. These results expose a gap between recognizing what is visible and correctly recovering the physical entities behind those observations.

## 6. Neighborhood Elites: Quality-Diversity Search for Uncertainty Quantification in Question Answering

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Mesut Ceylan, Arda Baris Basaran, Elgar Fleisch, Filipe Barata
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40278v1

**Abstract**

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly capable, yet they produce wrong answers indistinguishable from correct ones. In question answering (QA), such an error propagates unnoticed into downstream tasks. Perturbation-based uncertainty quantification (UQ) addresses this by probing a model over a set of input rewrites and reading uncertainty from how much its predictions vary. Yet this set is usually built generically, by free paraphrasing, token-level perturbation, or adversarial search. Such rewrites leave two things uncontrolled that matter in QA: what kind of change is made to the context, and how explicitly the context supports the answer. When neither is controlled, the composition of the set is determined by the generator rather than by design, and the estimate inherits whatever that generator happens to produce. Quality-diversity (QD) search is built to cover a predefined exploration space, so we recast input perturbation as a QD problem and propose \emph{neighborhood elites (NE)}, a neighborhood constructor that spreads answer-preserving rewrites across two axes, the kind of edit and the explicitness of support. We compare NE against three other neighborhood constructors, holding the editor, QA model, uncertainty measure and search objective fixed, and against three scores read from the original context. Across five extractive-QA datasets and five instruction-tuned LLMs, NE improves failure detection against every other constructor in at least 24 of the 25 dataset-model pairs, reaching 0.726 AUROC and 0.211 AUPRC lift over the base rate. Additionally, NE detects failure better than semantic entropy computed over decoder samples, by 0.074 AUROC and 0.062 AUPRC lift, and this advantage widens as the neighborhood grows.

## 7. Multistep and Multivariate Time Series Explainability with GradientSHAP: A Wind Power Case Study

- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40330v1

**Abstract**

As the effects of climate change become increasingly apparent, the demand for renewable energy sources such as wind power continues to grow. Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a powerful tool to address these issues. Still, its adoption in the energy sector has been hindered by the opacity of many AI models, which often function as black boxes. This paper introduces a carefully adapted GradientSHAP approach to provide interpretability for multivariate, multistep wind power forecasting -- a largely underexplored area. While GradientSHAP is not an out-of-the-box solution, our findings demonstrate that, with thoughtful handling of SHAP values and domain expertise, it can offer valuable insights into model behaviour, supporting more informed and trustworthy decision-making. ## 8. Multistatic ISAC with Deep Learning-Based Clutter Suppression for 5G and Beyond

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Yalin E. Sagduyu, Tugba Erpek, Sastry Kompella
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40586v1

**Abstract**

Integrated sensing and communications (ISAC) systems using 5G and beyond signals of opportunity enable joint wireless sensing and communication using shared infrastructure and spectrum resources. While multistatic ISAC can increase sensing coverage with spatially distributed receivers, dense and dynamic clutter can significantly degrade bistatic range and Doppler estimation, leading to errors in target localization and velocity estimation. This paper presents a multistatic ISAC framework with clutter suppression for high-fidelity estimation of target position and velocity. Distributed clutter is modeled with randomly located scatterers with varying Doppler spreads and radar cross sections to capture dense and dynamic clutter effects. A broad range of conventional signal processing baselines are evaluated for clutter suppression, showing limited robustness under strong clutter conditions. These methods substantially outperform conventional approaches in target position and velocity estimation across diverse clutter conditions in multistatic ISAC.

**Abstract**

Deep learning models for whole-body positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) hold significant clinical potential for disease detection, treatment planning, and response assessment. Although self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a promising approach to mitigating annotation scarcity, its application to whole-body PET/CT remains relatively underexplored due to the limited availability of large-scale datasets and the challenges associated with learning complementary cross-modal representations. ## 12. IMPACT: Inverter-Informed Multimodal Learning for Photovoltaic Generation Forecasting

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Anushka Katiyar, Cong Feng, Jing Wang, Tristan Liu, Simon Julien, Zachary Jacobs
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40418v1

**Abstract**

This work develops a physics-informed multimodal deep learning framework for 10- to 110-minute-ahead probabilistic photovoltaic power forecasting. The forecasting model is built on a four-branch architecture that separately encodes plant-level temporal history and inverter-level spatial cloud dynamics through a graph attention encoder with a learned adjacency, known-future solar geometry, and the current meteorological state, fused through gated cross-attention. The geometry branch adds two forward-looking physical signals, spatial cloud heterogeneity and inverter ramp-down activity, each empirically weighted toward the horizon where it is most predictive. A branch-composition ablation shows that every branch contributes positively and the model retains substantial skill in challenging overcast and variable sky conditions (FSS 0.198 and 0.255).

## 13. How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Max Neuwinger, Hanna Yukhymenko, Imanol Schlag
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516v1

**Abstract**

Safety alignment for Large Language Models is heavily focused on English with limited multilingual representation, particularly damaging low-resource language performance. Related open training datasets are often limited in size or do not follow region-localized harmful topics to broaden the scope of alignment beyond cultures. We propose a scalable template-based augmentation pipeline to produce massive multilingual refusal and benign corpora, extracting safe templates from harmful prompts, translating them and then filling in the slots with culturally authentic entities, both harmful and benign. ## 14. Every Token Needs Its Own Teacher: Multi-teacher Adaptive On-policy Self-distillation

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Yifu Luo, Chuang Ma, Zeyu Chen, Enkelejda Kasneci, Qi Chen, Gjergji Kasneci
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40574v1

**Abstract**

On-policy Self-distillation (OPSD) has proven effective for post-training large language models by constructing a teacher conditioned on privileged references. However, existing approaches keep the teacher fixed either throughout training by consistently exposing it to the full reference, or within each response by adaptively varying partial reference exposure only across responses. In this paper, we argue that neither of them is sufficient. Instead, every token needs its own teacher, as the teacher preference can vary token by token within the same response. Through a teacher-intervention pilot study, we validate that the teacher preference differs across tokens, and reveal that a preferred teacher can be identified for each token through majority voting and KL-based selection. Motivated by these findings, we propose Multi-teacher Adaptive On-policy Self-Distillation (MTA-OPSD), the first token-level adaptive OPSD method. Our method constructs a multi-teacher pool consisting of candidate teachers, and enables every token to adaptively select its preferred teacher during training. Experiments with Qwen3 models on mathematical reasoning tasks show that MTA-OPSD consistently outperforms existing OPSD approaches with an average gain of up to $+5.9$ over the base model, highlighting the importance of adapting teacher supervision at the token level. The code is available at https://github.com/xingzhejun/MTA-OPSD.

## 15. Don't Lose the Thread: Empowering Long-Horizon LLM Agents with Cognitive Resource Self-Allocation

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Zheng Zhu, Xiaozhuan Liang, Xi Chen, Bei Yu, Jiaya Jia
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40350v1

**Abstract**

Agents powered by large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable progress in solving complex reasoning tasks. However, LLM agents often falter on long-horizon tasks due to cognitive overload, as their working memory becomes cluttered with expanding and irrelevant information, which dilutes their attention and hinders effective planning and reasoning. To mitigate this challenge, we introduce Cognitive Resource Self-Allocation (CORAL), a novel reasoning paradigm that empowers agents to proactively optimize their context. Implemented as an agent-callable working memory management toolset, CORAL allows an agent to checkpoint its task progress and verified facts, and adaptively initiate a new problem-solving episode by resetting cluttered context while preserving only the checkpointed information, enabling it to resume reasoning from a clean state. We further enhance the agent's checkpointing capabilities using a Multi-episode Agentic Reinforced Policy Optimization algorithm. On the GAIA benchmark, CORAL with an 8B model achieves 42.8% average accuracy, with particularly strong gains on the most challenging long-horizon tasks (Level 2 and Level 3). Crucially, these gains come at a substantially lower inference cost: CORAL consumes 6-7$\times$ fewer input tokens than a ReAct agent. It also generalizes to multi-hop Knowledge QA benchmarks and to the considerably harder BrowseComp, where it improves a strong proprietary agent by 8.0 points.

## 16. Deep Learning Based Behavioral Drift Modeling for Continuous Biometric Authentication

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Suprava Saha Dibya, Minar, Abdulla Al Noman, Md. Zafir Abid Shresto, Ahmed Sayef
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40398v1

**Abstract**

This research proposes a two-level keystroke dynamics authentication system that provides defense-in-depth security through continuous behavioral drift monitoring. Level 1 employs fixed-text password authentication using Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) with contrastive learning and population-based training (PBT), achieving 3.77% EER for high-security login verification. Upon successful authentication, Level 2 continuously monitors user typing during natural free-text sessions using a 4-Model Deep Learning Ensemble. The final system achieves 96%+ accuracy for fixed-text authentication (3.77% EER) and 83.53% True Acceptance Rate (TAR) at 5% False Acceptance Rate (FAR) for free-text continuous authentication (10.05% EER), demonstrating superior performance and strong resistance to impostor attacks through continuous behavioral monitoring.

## 17. Conformal Annotation with Randomized Expert Review: Application to Online Cell-Type Labeling

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Xuhe Zhang, Zhi Huang, Ying Jin
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40252v1

**Abstract**

Artificial intelligence (AI) models can accelerate cell-type annotation in digital pathology, but safely scaling their use requires determining, with error control, which model-generated labels can be accepted without expert review. This is challenging in evolving workflows because cell morphology and type composition may change across tissue regions and slides, making one-time calibration unreliable. We propose Conformal Annotation with Randomized Expert review under Distribution shift (CARED), a distribution-free framework that leverages randomized expert audit to achieve long-run error control for AI-assisted annotation under distribution shift. For each incoming batch, CARED uniformly selects a subset of cases for expert review and uses the acquired labels to calibrate later decisions. Among unreviewed cases, CARED determines a subset of them to safely skip expert labeling, while suggesting candidate labels for the others for downstream resolution. We prove that this workflow provides long-run control of cumulative error over both the full stream and the cells outside of the audit sample, without stationarity or exchangeability assumptions. We further enhance the framework by introducing class-conditional guarantees to ensure error control within important but rare cell types, together with a utility module to target more automatic resolutions, smaller candidate sets, and greater biological coherence, without compromising validity. Experiments on pancreas, lung, and ovary annotation streams, together with a stream constructed from real expert annotations, show that CARED controls the cumulative errors while a substantial fraction of cells (up to 70%) bypass expert review.

## 19. Collaborative AI agents for autonomous experimental research with solid-state quantum spins

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** El Mustapha Mansouri, Eikichi Kimura, Masato Koga, Keigo Arai
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-29
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40589v1

**Abstract**

An autonomous research laboratory could conduct an entire study, from formulating questions to preparing manuscripts. Artificial intelligence (AI) offers one route towards this goal. Recent AI systems take on several research roles, but may draw conclusions that the evidence does not support. Here, we present an autonomous research laboratory in which agents based on large language models assess one another's work and can stop or redirect investigations. They can generate and reject research questions, require separate theoretical checks and conduct experiments designed to challenge their claims. **Abstract**

Artificial intelligence (AI)-based systems show promise for assisting primary care providers (PCPs) with patient care. **Abstract**

Artificial intelligence has high potential for impact in healthcare applications, but its training and deployment are challenging due to diverse data, a complex spectrum of possible tasks and important privacy needs. High-performing foundation models that enable data-efficient fine-tuning for diverse downstream tasks can meaningfully accelerate development in this domain. We introduce MedGemma, a collection of medical vision-language foundation models based on Gemma 3. MedGemma demonstrates advanced medical understanding and reasoning across images and text and multiple medical imaging domains, exceeding the performance of similarly sized generative models while maintaining the general capabilities of the Gemma base models. For out-of-distribution tasks, MedGemma achieves improvements of 2.6-10% in medical image question answering, 15.5-18.1% in chest X-ray finding classification and 10.8% in agentic evaluations compared with the base models. Our results show that fine-tuning MedGemma can be more effective than fine-tuning the base Gemma 3 model for medical tasks, particularly in the setting of limited training data. We additionally introduce MedSigLIP, a medically tuned vision encoder derived from SigLIP. MedSigLIP powers the visual understanding capabilities of MedGemma and, as an encoder, achieves performance comparable to or better than that of many specialized medical image encoders. Taken together, the MedGemma collection provides a strong foundation of medical image and text capabilities, with the potential to accelerate medical research and the development of downstream applications.

## 57. Governing the digital determinants of health for children and young people: a global public health imperative.

- **Journal:** Lancet (London, England)
- **Authors:** Prainsack B, Medhora RP, Kickbusch I
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-04
- **Source:** PubMed
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42830094/

**Abstract**

The digital determinants of health are the structural conditions through which digital infrastructures, business models, and design choices shape health outcomes. Yet these determinants remain largely outside public health policy and remain fragmented across regulatory sectors. Rather than regulating individual technologies in isolation, governance should ensure that digital systems and environments advance population health and reduce inequities. A public health approach shifts attention from individual responsibility-and the tendency to blame users-towards the governance of the structural features that produce health risks and inequities. We identify four governance domains for the digital determinants of health: infrastructure and access, platform design and commercial practices, data governance and surveillance, and institutional accountability and rights. Effectively governing these domains requires intersectoral strategies that tackle problematic concentrations of political and economic power. We argue that the governance of the digital determinants of health should be recognised as a core public health function, with the health of children and young people as a priority and entry point and precaution as a guiding governance principle.
