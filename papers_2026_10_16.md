## 1. When Reasoning Is Not Enough: Proactive Inquiry for Cross-Modal Safety in Multimodal Large Language Models

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Wei Cai, Jian Zhao, Yuchen Yuan, Xuelong Li
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22068v1

**Abstract**

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can act unsafely when individually benign images and texts are interpreted jointly. Beyond implicit reasoning risk, where reasoning drifts toward harm, we identify intent-level risk, where cross-modal ambiguity silently commits the model to a hazardous premise that path-level optimization alone cannot correct. We propose SAFE-PRO, which uses a Risk-Conditioned Uncertainty (RCU) signal, combining predictive entropy with a learned cross-modal risk score, to decide when to ask safety-grounded clarifying questions and where to apply path optimization, under a reward that prioritizes safety and penalizes over-refusal. We also build SSUI-Dialog, a multimodal multi-turn dataset with dual-intent supervision, and theoretically analyze gradient variance and unsafe-completion risk under RCU. Across seven benchmarks and two base MLLMs, SAFE-PRO consistently improves safety over SRPO, roughly halves its over-refusal, and surpasses GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet in average single-turn safety.

## 2. Training Reasoning Models to Generate Diverse Scientific Ideas

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Manya Wadhwa, Pranav Narayanan Venkit, Hiroaki Hayashi, Yu Li, Tuhin Chakrabarty, Chien-Sheng Wu
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22324v1

**Abstract**

One of the first steps of a research pipeline is generating ideas. Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used to automate this step, but most of this work optimizes the quality of individual ideas, while overlooking diversity. In our work, we study document-grounded ideation, focusing on increasing the diversity of LLM generated ideas and ensuring they are high quality. Given prior papers, a model must propose a set of ideas that are each high-quality and clearly distinct from one another, while also being grounded in the input documents. To optimize for this, we propose training with a set-level reinforcement learning objective. In our setup, in a single generation, the model writes K structured idea cards grounded in the source papers, and the set is scored jointly, where each idea's quality is weighted by its distance from the ideas written before it. This teaches the model to steer each new idea away from its predecessors. We show that our trained 8B model achieves higher creative utility (a measure combining quality and diversity) than Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-6 Astra on our contamination-free benchmark. In a pilot human study with four experts, our sets are judged as promising as Claude Opus 4.8's and contain marginally more unique ideas. When a coding agent runs proof-of-concept experiments on the ideas, our model's success@k is comparable to Claude Opus 4.8's, so we see that the added diversity does not cost any executability.

## 3. Towards Continual Learning: Mitigating Forgetting With Subspace Tuning

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Arnav Chavan, Kusha Sareen, Rishabh Tiwari, Abhranil Chandra, Johan Obando-Ceron, Kurt Keutzer, Scott Niekum, Inderjit S Dhillon, Devvrit Khatri
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22293v1

**Abstract**

Large language models (LLMs) acquire new skills through fine-tuning, but it can destroy capabilities they already have: catastrophic forgetting remains a central obstacle to continual learning in LLMs. Recent works connect forgetting to how far, and along which directions, fine-tuning moves the weights. On-policy methods such as reinforcement learning and on-policy distillation avoid updating the principal singular directions of weight matrices and thus forget less. In contrast, SFT concentrates updates along these directions and is susceptible to catastrophic forgetting. In this work, we introduce Subspace Tuning (SST), which restricts each weight update to the bottom singular subspace, eliminating changes along the principal directions. SST is optimizer-agnostic and composes well with fine-tuning recipes including SFT and self-distillation. SST combines tight subspace protection, dense gradient estimation, and a simple refresh mechanism, enabling stronger learning than prior orthogonal fine-tuning methods. With these mechanisms, protecting as much as 99\% of the weight subspace substantially improves retention while preserving strong target-task performance. This behavior strengthens with model and data size and nearly eliminates forgetting in the large-model regime. SST also keeps the model close to its base distribution, inducing $2$--$4\times$ less KL drift than the baseline recipes. Overall, SST advances the learning--forgetting Pareto frontier, making it a practical default for fine-tuning pre-trained language models.

## 4. Private Retrieval Augmented Generation via Random Projection

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Dixi Yao, Tahseen Rabbani, Tian Li
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22297v1

**Abstract**

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) enhances the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) by querying external structured knowledge. However, it can also introduce privacy risks by leaking sensitive information from the retrieval datastore. We propose a simple method to preserve datastore privacy in RAG systems via random projection. By applying the same projection to both datastore embeddings and query embeddings, our method provably preserves semantic similarity between queries and retrieved items while substantially mitigating data extraction attacks. Across multiple RAG architectures and datasets, we show that this lightweight approach achieves superior retrieval and generation performance compared to prior methods which leverage formal differential privacy (DP) guarantees, while exhibiting comparable empirical privacy under strong attack models. Our results for the first time suggest that random projection can serve as a competitive and practical baseline for privacy-preserving RAG systems.

## 5. PremiseSQL: Evaluating Premise Critique and Abstention in Text-to-SQL Systems

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Shuo Lin, Jinzhe Li, Chenlu Guo, Yanhan Li, Yuan Wu, Yi Chang
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22158v1

**Abstract**

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have substantially improved Text-to-SQL performance, yet existing NL2SQL systems largely assume that user queries are valid and answerable. As a result, models often generate executable SQL even for queries with flawed premises. We introduce premise critique ability in NL2SQL, namely the ability to recognize flawed queries and abstain from generating misleading SQL. To evaluate this capability, we propose PremiseSQL, a benchmark containing 1,800 flawed NL2SQL queries across four categories of premise failures, along with their 1,800 original queries. We further design an evaluation framework to measure intrinsic and prompt-induced premise-aware behavior across three prompting settings. Experiments on frontier LLMs show that current models are strongly premise-trusting by default and rarely critique flawed premises. Stronger critique-oriented prompting improves detection and abstention on flawed queries, but also increases false invalid judgments and suppresses SQL generation on originals. The code is available at https://github.com/vegeet/PremiseSQL.

## 6. Multi-sourced AI Tutor for Non-Native English Speaker Students

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Yiyin Shen, Xinyi Ai, Weibing Wang, Rogers Jeffrey Leo John, Meenakshi Syamkumar
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22163v1

**Abstract**

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being adopted as teaching tools in computing education, particularly for developing AI tutors capable of answering student questions. With the rise in enrollment of international students in computing programs, researchers have begun to explore the difficulties that non-native English speaker (NNES) students face in computing courses due to language barriers. To aid such students, we developed a general-purpose AI tutor system that can process student questions in foreign languages (currently focused on Mandarin) and provide bilingual responses. Our system also incorporates the functionality of generating a glossary of technical terms and their explanations to enhance comprehension. Our approach utilizes a specialized Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline with a novel question extraction mechanism, enabling us to effectively utilize a diverse array of course materials for answering student queries. We evaluated our AI tutor using 91 Piazza posts, demonstrating that it could answer 83.52% of the posts correctly and in a helpful manner. The tutor also provided high-quality translations for 76.92% of the questions and generated technical term glossaries for 64.47% of them. Our findings underscore the potential of multi-source RAG pipeline in enhancing AI tutor systems, offering support for non-native speakers that are challenging to provide with traditional supporting staff of a computing course.

## 7. MLExpert: Towards a Generalist Agentic Model for Autonomous Machine Learning Engineering

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Shangheng Du, Jinxin Shi, Shuaiyu Zhang, Yiming Wu, Junpeng Zhao, Shiyang Feng, Xin Li, Liang He, Xiangchao Yan, Bo Zhang
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22344v1

**Abstract**

Large language models (LLMs) are expanding from assisting scientific research and engineering to autonomously developing and improving AI systems, advancing AI for AI (AI4AI) as a pathway toward recursive self-improvement (RSI). Workflow-driven machine learning engineering (MLE) systems provide a practical path toward this pursuit through complex orchestration that manages long-horizon experimentation, while recent MLE systems adopt model-directed interaction, allowing models to autonomously organize experiments and adapt optimization strategies based on execution feedback. However, feedback from each experiment reflects several preceding decisions, and a long MLE run contains many such costly experiments. Training must therefore select useful supervision from long interaction trajectories and learn from intermediate outcomes. We propose a post-training method for autonomous long-horizon MLE optimization and apply it to train MLExpert, a 35B Mixture-of-Experts agentic model. We design the Kaggle-MLE Factory to construct executable tasks and an interaction harness for model-directed experimentation. Hierarchical agentic SFT transfers general tool-use and coding capabilities to MLE through outcome-guided supervision. Windowed Interaction RL (WIRL) treats the multi-turn decisions between successive experiment results as an execution window and assigns it a shared outcome-based reward. Asynchronous collection and policy correction support policy updates before the trajectory ends. On MLE-Bench Lite, MLExpert improves the average medal rate of the base model from 37.9% to 72.7%, achieving performance comparable to larger frontier models such as Kimi-K3 and GLM-5.3. Further experiments show that MLExpert improves performance on NatureBench Lite, agent scaffold optimization, and algorithm optimization, suggesting that the learned optimization capability generalizes to other tasks.

## 8. Librarian Agents: Training LLMs to Curate Long-Context Workspaces that Anticipate Future Queries

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Anikait Singh, Teresa Zhang, Yoonho Lee, Roshen Sanjay Nair, Aviral Kumar, Chelsea Finn
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22273v1

**Abstract**

Reasoning over long document contexts remains a challenge for large language models (LLMs). Standard retrieval methods are inherently reactive, bottlenecking agents with complex, cross-document reasoning at inference time, while precomputed structures like knowledge graphs often fall short because they rely on rigid heuristics. Instead, can we allow agents to perform anticipatory directed curation: learning to proactively reorganize unstructured corpora prior to inference to efficiently serve diverse future queries. We build on a key asymmetry in modern agentic harnesses (e.g., Claude Code): while LLM agents are adept at navigating existing filesystems to solve an immediate task, they are rarely trained to construct reusable workspaces for future use. To instantiate this capability, we introduce Librarian Agents, a framework in which a builder agent transforms raw documents into a hierarchical filesystem of synthetic files and reference links, which forms a workspace tailored for a downstream answering agent. We formulate workspace construction as a Markov decision process and optimize the builder policy via reinforcement learning to directly maximize downstream answering accuracy. Empirically, Librarian Agents outperforms the strongest retrieval and precomputation baselines by 33 percentage points on BrowseComp-Plus, amortizes upfront construction costs across repeated queries, and generalizes zero-shot to unseen domains on BRIGHT with a 31-point average accuracy improvement.

## 9. Learning Changes the World and Then What Was Learned has Changed: Training and Deploying Neural Networks in the Control Loop

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Vittorio Lippi
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22084v1

**Abstract**

Neural networks offer critical adaptability for controlling complex systems where nominal modeling proves intractable. However, when offline-trained neural networks are deployed within closed-loop control systems, their actions fundamentally alter the state distribution upon which they were trained. This paper introduces a unified taxonomy to categorize these sequential failure modes: the initial Closed-Loop Identification Error, the deployment-induced Action Error, and the continuous Tracking Error. Through discrete-time sensitivity analysis, this work formalizes how offline-learned parameters locally guarantee a trajectory divergence upon deployment, degrading performance due to local loss geometry. To mitigate this distribution shift, we propose actionable design policies centered on time-scale separated online adaptation. These mechanisms are validated through ablation studies on a nonlinear Duffing oscillator, demonstrating that bounded continuous learning successfully mitigates trajectory divergence whereas static deployments fail, offering concrete guidelines for practitioners deploying machine learning components in safety-critical feedback systems.

## 10. Grounding Generative Verification in 3D Content

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Shuanglin Li, Jiaying Lin, Shuquan Ye
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22360v1

**Abstract**

Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and 3D generation have expanded 3D understanding and synthesis. However, beyond visual fidelity, task-specific correctness grounded in 3D evidence remains a major challenge. We argue that building reliable 3D systems requires not only stronger generation and reasoning, but also verification feedback that identifies unmet requirements and explains their violations, allowing systems to reflect on and refine their outputs. Such feedback must integrate multi-view observations and distinguish genuine inconsistencies from projection-dependent appearances. We make three contributions. First, we introduce 3DVerifierBench, a benchmark spanning 10 tasks that evaluates decisions, rejection explanations, and responses to controlled changes in specifications and candidates. A human study reveals a 21.6 percentage-point gap between humans and the strongest tested model, GPT-6 Astra, while explanation-aware analysis exposes weaknesses in rejection reliability. Second, we analyze verification across fine-grained factual categories, exposing errors in values and target binding. We develop a scalable pipeline combining multi-view fact extraction, controlled 3D editing, and native annotations to produce 14,126 training examples with contradiction explanations and linked counterfactuals. Third, we examine how verification could support 3D generation through candidate selection and recursive refinement. In human-labeled sixteen-candidate pools, oracle selection improves complete factual success from 26.7% to 90.0%. In a separate eight-input study, human-guided recursive refinement satisfies all seven requirements for every input, compared with three under parallel Oracle@8. Together, our benchmark, analysis, and data pipeline establish a foundation for models to reflect on 3D content through reliable verification feedback.

## 11. Grid-Bench: Can LLMs Predict How Discrete Worlds Change?

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Zixiang Xu, Zirui Song, Wanrong Zheng, Chiyu Zhang, Yue Huang, Yuyang Peng, Kexin Zhang, Jinkun Chen, Xiuying Chen, Xiangliang Zhang, Yan Liu, Laurent Itti, Frank Shyu, Xiaoqiang Luo, Sandeep Pandey, Xi Liu, Luke Simon, Xiyang Hu, Yue Zhao
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22312v1

**Abstract**

Spatial instructions describe how to move objects, transform regions, or modify their surroundings. When several operations are applied in sequence, each acts on a configuration shaped by earlier changes. How accurately can large language models (LLMs) predict the resulting spatial state? We introduce Grid-Bench, an executable benchmark in which eight families of operations transform discrete worlds represented as two-, three-, and four-dimensional grids. Given an initial grid, explicit rules, and an event history expressed as text, a model predicts the complete final grid. Reference execution makes every predicted value checkable, supporting comparisons across spatial operations, history lengths, and workloads. Evaluating six LLMs shows that complete-state accuracy falls sharply as histories grow, with substantial divergence among models that perform similarly on single events. Models with similar exact-match accuracy can nevertheless differ substantially in the number of incorrect cells on shared instances. Even over long histories, some models recover many correct changes beyond what copying the initial state would yield. Grid-Bench makes both complete recovery and these differences in returned states directly measurable across a range of spatial conditions.

## 12. Generalization Guarantees of Randomized Block Coordinate Descent for Nonconvex Learning

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Ruixi Hu, Liang Wu
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22213v1

**Abstract**

Randomized block coordinate descent (RBCD) is a popular optimization algorithm for many large-scale machine learning problems. The existing theoretical analysis of RBCD mainly focuses on its convergence behavior from an optimization perspective, while its generalization behavior remains less understood for nonconvex problems. In this paper, we establish high-probability generalization guarantees for RBCD with nonconvex objectives. In the general nonconvex setting, we derive bounds on the gradients of both empirical and population risks, requiring only standard smoothness assumptions. With polynomially decaying step sizes, we tune the iteration number to balance optimization and generalization, thereby quantifying how early stopping leads to the optimal convergence rate for the population gradients. In the gradient-dominated setting, we provide bounds in terms of functional suboptimality, which nearly match the optimistic rates in convex and low-noise settings. Finally, we develop dimension-independent bounds for loss functions of a specific structure, making the generalization guarantees applicable to high-dimensional problems.

## 14. Explaining the Roles of Experts in Mixture-of-Experts LLMs for Personalization

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Sekh Mainul Islam, Pepa Atanasova, Isabelle Augenstein
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22127v1

**Abstract**

Personalization of Large Language Models (LLMs) is increasingly important for user-centric applications, yet prior methods mainly adapt dense LLMs, whose polysemantic neurons, each encoding multiple unrelated concepts, hinder localizing the components behind personalization, and thus understanding and controlling it. We propose to personalize Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs, which sparsely route each token to a few specialized experts, reducing polysemanticity and enabling such localization. We adapt three open-weight MoE LLMs on a large-scale personalization dataset and train crosscoders on paired activations of the personalized and non-personalized models to learn interpretable personalized latents, from which we identify the experts encoding distinct personalization features. The personalized MoE LLMs perform competitively with dense LLMs while activating only a fraction of their parameters, exhibit lower polysemanticity than dense LLMs with similar active parameters, and, within the same model family, yield personalized latents that are less shared with the non-personalized model. Causal interventions show that these latents suffice to recover the personalized model's behavior and that boosting their associated experts moves the non-personalized model toward it, revealing distinct routing paths for different personalization features.

## 15. Evaluation Awareness Beyond English: Do LLMs Act Differently When Tested in Indic Languages?

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Trizal Garg, Sibayan Mitra, Arya Karanjkar, Advait Singh, Jagat Sesh Challa, Yash Sinha, Siddharth Mehrotra, Dhruv Kumar, Pratham Yogish Maliya
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22333v1

**Abstract**

Large language models (LLMs) can often tell when they are being tested. This ability, known as evaluation awareness, can lead models to act more cautiously during safety evaluations than in real use. Existing work has studied evaluation awareness almost entirely in English, although models serve users in many languages. Indic languages are spoken by over a billion people, yet evaluation awareness remains almost entirely unstudied in them. We test four instruction-tuned models from three families in five Indic languages alongside English. A set of cues varies whether the model is told that it is monitored and whether the monitoring is a safety test. Models refuse harmful requests more often when they are told that they are being tested. This increase appears in all four models and all six languages. In Indic languages, refusal of harmful requests rises by 14.4 percentage points on average. The test cue also brings Indic refusal of harmful requests level with English, but raises refusal of benign Indic requests from 6.9% to 11.3%. We release all 47,880 judged responses to support further work. These results suggest that safety evaluations in Indic languages may not reflect how models treat their everyday users.

## 16. DropKV: Decoupling Residual-Output Perturbation for Near-Optimal KV-Cache Eviction

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Aozhong Zhang, Selcuk Gurses, Yanxia Deng, Naigang Wang, Chi-Chun Liu, Davis Wertheimer, Derrick Liu, Xin Li, Zi Yang, Felix X. -F. Ye, Penghang Yin
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22130v1

**Abstract**

Key-value (KV) cache management has become a critical bottleneck for scaling Large Language Models (LLMs) to long-context scenarios due to linear memory growth. While recent eviction methods have moved beyond simple attention-score heuristics to incorporate value representations, they typically face a fundamental trade-off: the underlying optimization for minimizing output perturbation is an NP-hard combinatorial problem, leading to mathematically suboptimal heuristics or implementations that are difficult to kernelize. In this work, we propose DropKV, a simple principled eviction framework based on Decoupled Residual-Output Perturbation. By decoupling the joint eviction decision into independent per-token scoring, DropKV circumvents combinatorial intractability and admits a provable constant-factor approximation guarantee under a cone condition that we empirically verify on three long-context LLMs. To ensure practical viability, we provide a high-performance implementation using fused Triton kernels that avoid materializing the full attention matrix, delivering a 19.8x scoring-kernel speedup that translates to up to a 9.9% matched-batch end-to-end prefill speedup. Extensive evaluations on the RULER and LongBench benchmarks demonstrate that DropKV consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines at equivalent cache budgets, achieving the lowest inference latency among eviction methods while also remaining faster than the dynamic cache baseline.

## 17. Discovering state-of-the-art quantum circuits with large language models

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Andi Gu, Alek H. Kemeny, Susanne F. Yelin, Mikhail D. Lukin
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22367v1

**Abstract**

Significant efforts are currently being directed toward the implementation of large-scale quantum information processors, but realizing useful quantum advantage also requires the development of efficient quantum algorithms. While for many practical problems the dominant resource costs are associated with a small set of computational primitives, optimizing them in the quantum setting presents unique challenges, including a reversibility constraint absent from ordinary classical computation. Here we introduce an evolutionary framework in which large language models (LLMs) propose circuit designs and an independent evaluator verifies and scores them. We apply this framework to tasks spanning numerical computation, chemistry, optimization, quantum simulation, and number theory. In seven of the eight tasks, the discovered circuits significantly reduce both Toffoli and qubit counts relative to the state of the art. In the eighth, the search discovers a new reversible architecture for prime-field modular inversion. By handling reversibility differently, the architecture makes divsteps -- a classical algorithm previously impractical for reversible integer arithmetic -- viable and substantially improves the Toffoli-qubit trade-off for modular inversion. These results open the door for evaluator-guided LLM co-design of new algorithmic structures for quantum computation.

## 18. Designing Transformers for Continuous Diffusion Language Models

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Weijian Luo, Yujian Chen, Hao Jiang, Guang Cheng, Tianyang Hu
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22330v1

**Abstract**

Continuous diffusion language models (CDLMs) offer an alternative approach to parallel language generation; however, most existing studies directly use transformer architectures from either causal large language models (LLMs) or Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) for image generation, which were not optimized for CDLMs. In this paper, we conduct a systematic study on Transformer architectures for CDLMs and introduce Language Diffusion Transformer (LangDiT), combining feature and query-key normalization, wider noise conditioning, likelihood-shortcut gradient routing and calibration, and confidence-gated training self-conditioning. LangDiT significantly reduces generation perplexity in matched LangFlow comparisons, establishing a new state of the art in continuous language generation across different model formulations. Using LangFlow as a representative CDLM, LangDiT as a plug-and-play backbone leads to a new state-of-the-art model with a generation perplexity (GenPPL) of 21.72 using 1024 NFEs and 44.72 GenPPL using 128 NFEs on the unconditional generation of 1024-token sequences on the OpenWebText dataset. When adapted to Plaid-style CDLM formulations, LangDiT results in a new leading GenPPL of 11.33 using 1024 denoising NFEs, and 27.51 GenPPL using 128 denoising NFEs. These gains are achieved while retaining each model's underlying representation, training objective, and inference sampler, isolating transformer architecture as a fundamental source of improvement. Our study identifies architectural design as an important source of improvement in continuous diffusion language models.

## 19. Denoising Textures is a Different Ballgame Altogether!

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Saptarshi Mandal, Oindrila Haldar, Chirayata Bhattacharya, Chandra Sekhar Seelamantula, Zhiwei Xu, Thrasyvoulos N. Pappas, Gaurav Sharma
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22258v1

**Abstract**

The human brain effortlessly deciphers colors and textures from visual stimuli and reconstructs the world around us, yet preserving such details remains challenging in image denoising. While significant progress has been made in generic image denoising, the problem of texture denoising has remained relatively under-explored. Fine details, such as intricate patterns and repetitive structures, often suffer from over-smoothing even in deep learning based approaches for denoising. In this paper, we analyze the behavior of state-of-the-art (SOTA) denoisers on texture datasets and show that performance improvements are possible only by fine-tuning the denoisers on the texture datasets. These findings highlight the need for specialized treatment of textures in denoising. Since denoising quality directly impacts downstream tasks such as generative modelling, we argue that dedicated texture aware denoisers are essential for faithful texture reconstruction and synthesis.

**Abstract**

Despite growing interest in the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in medicine, empirical evidence on how clinicians use these tools is limited. To characterize clinician use of an AI-enabled clinical information platform, the distribution of use across the clinical workforce, and the purpose of submitted queries. ## 21. Change the Number, Change the Answer: Assessing Rating Scale Sensitivity in LLM-Based Text Scoring

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Wonjeong Jo, Gyuwan Kim, Ming Shan Hee
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22187v1

**Abstract**

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used in computational social science to assign scalar scores to social and psychological constructs. However, studies use different rating scales, and whether their scores remain comparable across scale designs is understudied. ## 24. Baselines and Score Centering in Policy Gradient Estimation

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Shuo Han, Ziqing Hu
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-15
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22263v1

**Abstract**

Reinforcement learning has become a widely used approach to post-training large language models. In a typical implementation, learning is off-policy: The policy generating the responses differs from the policy being trained because of training-inference mismatch or policy staleness in asynchronous training. This discrepancy is commonly addressed by importance sampling, whose ratio is usually regularized in practice through truncation, masking, or clipping. Under regularized importance sampling, the importance-weighted policy gradient is no longer invariant to constant shifts in the reward. This invariance is recovered by using the covariance between the reward and the importance-weighted score as the estimand, which coincides with the policy gradient when the ratio is not regularized. This note develops this covariance perspective for both sequence-level and token-level importance sampling. The covariance perspective reveals the roles of the group-mean baseline and score centering in policy gradient estimation. Both perform centering in covariance estimation, but they differ in the mean being subtracted: The group-mean baseline uses a sample mean, whereas score centering uses a population mean. The two lead to distinct unbiased covariance estimators with different variances. Whether both forms of centering should be used simultaneously depends on whether importance sampling is applied at the sequence level or at the token level. With sequence-level importance sampling, score centering adds no benefit once the group-mean baseline is used; with token-level importance sampling, using the group-mean baseline without score centering generally introduces bias when the importance ratio is regularized.

**Abstract**

Objectives To evaluate the performance of large language models (LLMs) in risk of bias assessment and to examine whether prompt engineering improves their accuracy and alignment with expert reasoning. Methods We analysed 158 randomised controlled trials from 10 dental systematic reviews and their risk of bias assessments were reviewed and revised to serve as the reference standard. Two LLMs (DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-5) were evaluated under four prompting strategies, including direct command, command with reference, constrained output and formula-constrained output. The direct command served as the blank control group, simulating the approach commonly used by clinicians, whereas the other three groups employed different prompt engineering. The performance of LLMs across the seven domains of RoB-1 was evaluated using accuracy and agreement. The reasoning process of the LLMs was expressed in the form of syllogisms and its similarity to expert reasoning was assessed using MMD 2 . Results LLMs showed limited capability in risk of bias assessment under the blank control condition, with mean accuracies of 0.72 for DeepSeek-V3 and 0.65 for GPT-5. With formula-constrained prompting, the performance of both LLMs improved significantly, and the overall accuracy increased to 0.85 for both DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-5 (both vs the blank control group, p<0.001). Agreement metrics showed a similar pattern, with higher agreement under formula-constrained prompting than under the other prompting strategies (p<0.001 for both models). In addition, the syllogistic output format provided a clear representation of the reasoning process underlying risk of bias assessment. Compared with constrained output, formula-constrained prompting also produced reasoning that was more closely aligned with the reference answers, as indicated by lower MMD² values (DeepSeek-V3: 0.0765 vs 0.1239; GPT-5: 0.0548 vs 0.1068). Conclusion Prompt engineering substantially improved the performance of LLMs in risk of bias assessment. Although LLMs cannot currently replace human reviewers, they may serve as efficient and transparent tools to support this process.
