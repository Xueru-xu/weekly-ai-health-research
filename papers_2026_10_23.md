## 1. Who Is Sycophantic to Whom? Confident LLMs, Human Deference, and the Collapse of Trust Calibration

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Deepthika S, Pawni Yadav, B. Sankar
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31579v1

**Abstract**

Research on sycophancy asks whether large language models (LLMs) bend towards what their users want to hear. We ask the reverse question: when an LLM is confident, consistent, and wrong, do users bend towards it? In a within-subjects experiment, 32 participants learned up to five science topics by chatting with five assistants built on the same model (GPT-5-mini). Unknown to participants, each assistant's briefing had 0, 25, 50, 75, or 100% of its facts replaced by plausible falsehoods, and every assistant spoke with full confidence. A surprise quiz after each topic offered the correct fact, the planted falsehood, and two distractors, with a confidence rating for every answer. Across 104 quizzes and 1,040 answers, scores fell from 7.4 to 2.9 out of 10, and the planted option was chosen on 60% of falsified items against 11% of correctly taught ones (odds ratio 12.5). Transcript coding showed that whenever the assistant stated a falsehood, it was adopted about 65% of the time at every falsification level. Absorbed falsehoods were held with high confidence (8.4/10), the modeled confidence advantage of right over wrong answers vanished, and the discrimination of confidence for correctness fell from an AUROC of 0.70 to chance, while post-task trust ratings barely moved. Participants tended to return to the chat more when the assistant was worse, but returning went with more absorption, not less, and 30% of all turns pasted quiz questions back to the source being checked. The assistant itself was almost never sycophantic and never conceded under pushback. We introduce a baseline-corrected Human Deference Index to quantify this human-side sycophancy and argue that trustworthiness is a property of the human-AI pair, that sycophancy evaluations should measure both directions, and that learning interfaces must make reliability legible claim by claim.

## 2. When LLMs Forget What They Believe: Characterizing State Integrity Collapse in Long-Horizon Dialogues

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Yancheng Zhu, Yifan Yuan
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31314v1

**Abstract**

Large language models can repeat an instruction while failing to keep obeying it. We study this recall-compliance dissociation in long-horizon dialogue and define State Integrity Collapse: a model preserves a stated constraint in memory but stops applying it to later turns. We introduce StateTrack-Bench, a controlled suite of conversations covering numerical, format, role/persona, factual-memory, and logical-rule constraints. Across seven provider-pinned model snapshots and five dialogue-depth probes, strict joint retention ranges from 62.4% to 100.0%, with degradation concentrated in symbolic constraints and in one especially brittle model. A forked recall audit shows that 88.6% of observed failures are behavioral-drift failures rather than ordinary forgetting; excluding the brittle outlier preserves this diagnosis (84.4% of failures). Extended evaluations at depths up to 50 turns confirm that the dissociation persists beyond the core benchmark, while Structured State Anchoring recovers up to 24.4 points on immutable constraints. The results show that reliability cannot be inferred from single-turn instruction following alone; evaluation must track whether remembered constraints remain operational as dialogue state grows. Code and artifacts are available at: https://github.com/xzzvsxd/StateTrack.

## 3. VERA: A Responsible AI Dashboard for Assessing LLM Deployments in Light of the EU AI Act

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Robin Quériaux, Solveig Bachellery, Léa Déléris, Saurabhsingh Rajput, Mariam Barry, Tushar Sharma, Jérôme Lebecq
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31588v1

**Abstract**

Institutions that deploy large language models in regulated settings often need technical evidence of responsible AI practices before approving a deployment. COMPL-AI provides a technical interpretation of the EU AI Act that organises this evidence under six principle groups and eighteen technical requirements, twelve of which current benchmarks can measure. The benchmark suites that produce this evidence are typically operated from a command line, while the staff who approve deployments are non-engineers. We present VERA, an open-source self-hosted dashboard deployed to facilitate responsible AI processes within a large European bank. A reviewer selects a model and runs public benchmarks against it. VERA scores the twelve requirements that current benchmarks can measure and instruments the energy consumed by each evaluation, adding run-level evidence for environmental impact. We run four served open-weight models through the requirement pipeline, and the dashboard displays a score for each measurable requirement. A within-subject study then compares how participants read these benchmark results: from the raw output files in one condition, and through the VERA dashboard in the other. The dashboard reduced the time needed to perform the responsible AI assessment, reducing the time required from 56 seconds using raw files to 30 seconds using our dashboard, on average. Our dashboard scored highly (4.36 out of 5) on usefulness in the survey conducted within the organization. The dashboard and the study material are released publicly to promote replicability and reproducibility in different organisational contexts.

## 5. SympFormer: Inertial Attention Blocks from Damped Hamiltonian Dynamics on Spaces of Probability Measures

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Viktor Stein, Levin Maier, Wuchen Li, Gabriele Steidl
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31600v1

**Abstract**

The attention mechanism underpins modern large language models, yet their dynamics remain only partially understood. Recent perspectives interpret attention blocks as interacting particle systems, whose mean-field limits correspond to gradient flows of energy functionals on spaces of probability densities on $\mathbb R^d$ equipped with Wasserstein-type metrics. We extend this viewpoint by deriving accelerated attention blocks from inertial, Nesterov-type damped Hamiltonian dynamics on these density spaces, developing the geometry and differential calculus needed to make the construction rigorous. In particular, we introduce a local-mobility geometry for the Otto-Wasserstein and softmax-Wasserstein settings, and a quotient Stein geometry that resolves the degeneracy of the linear-attention kernel to yield a well-defined metric and differential calculus. We provide rigorous foundations when softmax attention admits a gradient-flow formulation and when its inertial counterpart admits a damped Hamiltonian on explicitly characterized domains. For linear attention, we obtain a finite-dimensional solution theory with sharp conditions for global existence versus finite-time second-moment blow-up. We further prove a quantitative link between continuous moment blow-up and the discrete-layer depth at which token norms cross a prescribed threshold. Finally, motivated by the finite-token geometry, we propose geometric corrections to token and momentum updates. In decoder-only, nanoGPT-style experiments on TinyStories and OpenWebText, these corrections lower validation negative log-likelihood on next-token prediction relative to momentum-only controls.

## 6. Social-HUMINT: An LLM-Augmented Unified Framework for Measuring Social Media Exposure

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Pellegrino Casoria, Simon Pietro Romano
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31573v1

**Abstract**

In the post-API landscape, social-media profiling has become inexpensive and automated: Large Language Models (LLMs) can extract structured information from unstructured content and deduce attributes users never disclosed. A person's true exposure is not what they post but what can be inferred from it. As these practices become routine, exposure to social engineering attacks becomes an operational certainty, demanding a shared framework for assessment and mitigation. We make this exposure measurable by quantifying how accurately a target can be profiled, and how far that profile enables social engineering attacks. Because no existing model fully spans this continuum, we unify Social Media Intelligence (SOCMINT) and Cyber Human Intelligence (Cyber-HUMINT), two individual-focused disciplines that evolved separately, into a single doctrine-grounded taxonomy of 38 capabilities across the Cyber Threat Intelligence lifecycle, where user profiling bridges passive collection and active engagement. Domain experts validated the taxonomy (Fleiss' $κ$ = 0.92). We operationalize its core path (extraction, inference, and susceptibility assessment) as an LLM-augmented multi-agent pipeline, evaluated on real participants' public data. Susceptibility is assessed indirectly: the pipeline predicts how each target would rank personalized engagement options, matching their actual choices significantly above chance. This ranking capacity captures the personalization on which tailored attacks depend. The underlying profiles are accurate: extraction reaches F1 = 97.3%, and inferred attributes score 4.73/5, both far above rule-based and NER-based baselines at negligible cost. The framework augments security analysts' tradecraft rather than replacing it and enables defenders to gauge their own exposure before an adversary does.

## 7. SQuAT: Spherical Lattice Vector Quantization-Aware Training

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Sebastian Loeschcke, Ivan Koryakovskiy, Tycho F. A. van der Ouderaa, Riccardo Del Chiaro, Mart van Baalen, Paul N. Whatmough, Markus Nagel
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31412v1

**Abstract**

Vector quantization (VQ), in particular lattice-based methods, offer strong rate-distortion trade-offs for low-bit large language models (LLMs), yet their behavior under quantization-aware training (VQ-QAT) remains poorly understood. We scale lattice VQ-QAT to billion-parameter LLMs and multi-billion-token training regimes, and introduce SQuAT, a shape-gain VQ-QAT approach trained with a separate straight-through estimator (STE) for the shape and gain. SQuAT decomposes weights into direction and magnitude: normalized directions are quantized using an $E_8$-derived spherical codebook, while gains are quantized independently with low-bit scalar quantizers. We show that, unlike a standard STE applied to the full quantizer, which ignores the shape-gain structure, these separate STEs induce tangent-space updates for directions, yielding an implicit form of spherical (Riemannian) optimization. To enable scalability, we develop a distributed exact nearest-neighbor assignment scheme, in which workers quantize disjoint weight blocks and communicate only discrete indices, reducing VQ-QAT training time by 12.8$\times$ and allowing us to scale it to 10B tokens. Across instruction-tuned LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B, Qwen3-4B, and base Qwen3-1.7B, SQuAT consistently outperforms standard VQ-QAT STE under identical quantizers and initialization, and achieves SOTA VQ-QAT accuracy.

## 8. Rethinking Sequence Compression with Test-time Regression

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Sihan Yang, Jie Huang, Hanxuan Li, Yifan Guo, Weigao Sun, Yiran Zhong, Weiyang Liu
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31574v1

**Abstract**

Sequence compression is a fundamental operation and a critical information bottleneck in efficient language and multimodal models, and yet prevailing approaches based on pooling or learned projections remain underperforming. We revisit sequence compression through the lens of test-time regression: input tokens define a regression problem whose fitted coefficients form the compressed representation. Importantly, the regression problem is learned end-to-end via backpropagation, while its coefficients are computed efficiently in closed form during the forward pass. This leads to TTR-Compressor, a general alternative to conventional pooling and projection-based compression. We evaluate TTR-Compressor as the compressor for block-wise sparse attention and the visual token merger for multimodal large language models. On block-wise sparse attention, TTR-Compressor improves RULER performance over attention-based compressors by a large margin. For visual token merging, it consistently outperforms the standard MLP merger and matches its performance with 55.6% fewer visual tokens. These results establish test-time regression as an effective primitive for sequence compression in efficient language and multimodal models.

## 9. Reliable AI-Assisted Decisions via Selective Auditing

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Isaac Gibbs, Elise L. Han, Ryan J. Tibshirani, Tijana Zrnic
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31662v1

**Abstract**

We consider online monitoring of an AI system that produces a sequence of decisions. At each round, the system outputs a proposed decision together with a confidence score estimating the trustworthiness of the decision. The decision and confidence score need not be produced by the same model: for example, one agent may propose a decision, while a separate verifier agent evaluates it and outputs a correctness or safety score. Based on this information, we must decide whether to accept the AI decision automatically or consult an expert, such as a human auditor, who reviews the proposal and either accepts or corrects it. We formulate this task as an online constrained optimization problem in which the goal is to minimize the frequency of expert auditing while guaranteeing that the fraction of AI errors that go unaudited stays below a user-defined threshold. We derive a family of algorithms with provable error-control guarantees with no assumptions, including a method that is optimally efficient: it asymptotically attains the best achievable auditing rate for both adversarial and i.i.d. data streams. Experiments on screening harmful requests to large language models, qualitative text annotation, and data labeling show that our methods achieve valid error control in practice while substantially reducing the need for human auditing compared to baselines.

## 10. Predictable performance is suboptimal, and what it means for model routing

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Guanhua Zhang, Moritz Hardt
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31428v1

**Abstract**

Large language models (LLMs) differ in capabilities and costs, motivating routing methods that adaptively choose a model for each query. These methods rely on performance prediction: given a query, estimate whether a model will answer it correctly. Most routing methods train separate predictors of candidate models' performance to guide model selection. Our work shows that performance prediction only beats a simple baseline when the candidate model is suboptimal on the task or the performance predictor adds significant capacity relative to the candidate model. Specifically, we take a model's self-confidence, the probability it assigns to its chosen answer, as a baseline and study how much a performance predictor can improve on it. First, we show that any performance predictor that outperforms a model's self-confidence can be used to improve the model's loss: the predictor's advantage transfers exactly in log loss through recalibration. Second, accounting for the predictor's additional parameters, we bound its advantage by the model's suboptimality plus the gain from those parameters. Third, we extend our theoretical results to two-model cascades, which route queries to a more capable model when the first model is predicted to fail. Experiments with 16 language models on four benchmarks show that predictors gain a larger advantage over self-confidence on more suboptimal models. This advantage shrinks as models improve through finetuning. Cascading exhibits the same two patterns: predictor-based routing offers larger gains over confidence-based routing for more suboptimal models, and these gains diminish after finetuning.

## 13. Muffin - A Shared Memory Buffer for Asynchronous AI Agents

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Ping-Lin Kuo, Suman Navaratnarajah, Christian Frey, Gresa Shala, Josif Grabocka
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31355v1

**Abstract**

The rise of Agentic AI systems has changed the landscape of artificial intelligence by enabling strong reasoning capabilities through specialized multi-agent interaction with structured self-reflection. However, in target-driven reasoning tasks, single- and multi-agent systems are often on par in terms of performance gains. A major challenge is that failures often arise from an ineffective exchange of critical information during inter-agent interaction and coordination. ## 14. Harmful Responses Are Jailbreak Templates: Transferring Autoregressive Jailbreaks to Diffusion Language Models

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Jinwei Gan, Zifeng Cheng, Zhiwei Jiang, Ran Sun, Shiping Ge, Qing Gu
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31331v1

**Abstract**

Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) enable flexible masked generation through bidirectional context modeling, but this capability also introduces a distinct jailbreak attack surface. We find that a harmful response to a given query naturally serves as an effective jailbreak template: simply masking a subset of its tokens can induce a dLLM to reconstruct harmful content. Based on this observation, we propose RMT-Jailbreak, a general framework that transfers existing autoregressive (AR) jailbreak attacks to dLLMs through their induced harmful responses. RMT-Jailbreak converts an AR-generated harmful response into a high-mask-ratio template using uniform token retention, and further applies prompt-based safety filtering to mask any remaining visible harmful content. The refined template is then provided to the target dLLM for bidirectional reconstruction. Extensive experiments across multiple AR jailbreak methods and target dLLMs demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of RMT-Jailbreak, revealing strong cross-paradigm transferability of jailbreak attacks.

## 15. From Structured Records to Patient Narratives: Preserving Clinical Alignment in LLM-Generated Health Summaries

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Bayram Ayadi, Florian Geissler, Johanna Schmidhuber
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31467v1

**Abstract**

As wearable and digital health systems increasingly generate longitudinal physiological records, communicating these observations in patient-accessible language remains a significant challenge. Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a promising mechanism for translating structured clinical information into readable narratives, but this process may introduce semantic drift, where clinically precise meaning is distorted during simplification. We investigate evidence-grounded health summarization for menstrual health monitoring using a constrained generation framework that combines structured evidence conditioning with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) using a prompt-based constrained generation framework. ## 18. Deep Learning for Random Objects in Metric Spaces: Beyond Manifold Assumptions

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Marcos Matabuena, Xiaokai Luo, Oscar Hernan Madrid Padilla
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31328v1

**Abstract**

Deep learning (DL) methods have substantially improved predictive performance across a wide range of downstream prediction tasks, enabling new discoveries in multiple domains. However, principled DL methods for non-Euclidean random objects remain relatively scarce, despite the growing importance of modern biomedical outcomes taking the form of probability distributions, random graphs, functions, and other random objects in metric spaces. Motivated by a new generation of digital and clinical biomarkers, we propose a deep learning framework for regression with random objects in metric spaces. Our approach operates directly on the metric structure of the response space, avoiding restrictive manifold or Euclidean embedding assumptions imposed by many existing methods. Extensive simulation studies demonstrate competitive predictive performance, with gains over several competing approaches in multiple Euclidean and non-Euclidean configurations. Finally, we demonstrate the practical utility of the proposed framework in two digital health case studies with distribution-valued outcomes in Wasserstein spaces, using high-resolution accelerometry and continuous glucose monitoring data. Embedding-based deep-learning methods may be less suitable when no informative low-dimensional representation exists or when the learned embedding does not adequately preserve the intrinsic geometry of the response space.

## 20. Cost-Aware Abstention for LLM-Based IT Ticket Classification

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Sachin Gupta
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-22
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31657v1

**Abstract**

Wrong automatic routing of an IT support ticket can cost more than human triage. We evaluate cost-aware abstention as an offline, cost-conditional readiness assessment: select a routing policy on calibration data under explicit costs, then evaluate that frozen policy once on held-out test data. On a public 47,837-ticket, eight-class corpus, we compare four trained non-LLM classifiers with four large language models under zero- and few-shot prompting. Under the illustrative cost model (c_ok, c_triage, c_wrong) = (0, 1, 5), TF-IDF with logistic regression and a threshold of 0.60 selected on calibration reduces test cost from 0.763 to 0.472, a 38.1% relative reduction (paired ratio-bootstrap 95% CI [35.7%, 40.4%]), while lowering wrong routes from 15.3% to 3.5%. Frozen calibration-selected policies also beat both always-route and always-defer on held-out test data in 14 of 16 settings in a post-hoc cost-parameter analysis. The strongest prompted configuration reaches 0.477 accuracy on a stratified 1,000-ticket test subset, versus 0.879 for fine-tuned DistilBERT on the same tickets. Isotonic lowers expected calibration error without reliably improving discrimination: GPT-4o-mini few-shot has one confidence value before and after recalibration and correctness AUROC 0.500. We therefore report correctness AUROC and tie-aware risk-coverage area alongside ECE. At the analytically derived break-even probability 0.80, seven prompted configurations defer all tickets; Haiku few-shot routes only 19/1,000.
