## 2. XStack: A Benchmark for ML Repo Migration

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Linrong Cai, Taiming Lu, Haozhe Chen, Zhuang Liu
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08472v1

**Abstract**

ML code should run where researchers choose, not where it was first written. Today, machine learning code mainly lives in two ecosystems, PyTorch on NVIDIA GPUs and JAX on Google TPUs. Migrating repositories between them means building and testing across frameworks and hardware. This migration is (1) a practical need, since it unlocks different hardware for researchers, and (2) a natural benchmark: it involves different frameworks and hardware, covers whole repositories, and is long-horizon and challenging. We introduce XStack, a benchmark that evaluates agents on migrating ML repositories between these two ecosystems. Each agent receives a PyTorch repository and a clean TPU machine and must deliver a JAX repository. Its output passes if it meets our grading criteria, based on the source repository's published result, authenticated and calibrated by us. Migration by agents remains unreliable: Claude Opus 5 passes 75%, GPT-5.6-sol 45%, and every other agent at most 40%. XStack is a step toward freeing ML code from its ecosystem. We release XStack to make progress toward reliable migration across frameworks and hardware measurable.

## 3. The Gold Rush in AI4Math, II: What are signatures of AI-assisted Proofs?

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Yuanchuan Guo, Jiashun Jin, Zheng Tracy Ke, Bingcheng Sui
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08412v1

**Abstract**

The growing use of AI in mathematics raises a question: can we detect AI involvement in proofs, separately from AI assistance with writing? Historical arXiv papers, recent papers disclosing substantive AI use, and manuscripts released by OpenAI provide a rich empirical basis for studying this question. However, several challenges remain: lack of matched case-control pairs; potential incompleteness of disclosure; and lack of ready-to-use features designed to capture signals of AI involvement in proofs. To address these challenges, we construct five cohorts comprising 8,281 papers by combining existing manuscripts with those we produced using generative AI. These cohorts span different levels of AI involvement and include matched pairs of papers addressing the same research problems. We then introduce ProofTrace, a framework that represents each paper as a graph of immediate proof support (GIPS) and extracts interpretable features of mathematical organization. Our study yields three discoveries. First, principal-component projections of our features reveal a qualitative gradient of AI involvement, where AI-reworded papers remain close to human papers, the two AI-generated cohorts depart in broadly shared directions, and papers disclosing substantive AI use occupy intermediate positions which shifts toward AI-generated cohorts over time. Second, we identify recurring signatures of AI-generated proofs, such as fewer externally cited results, greater reliance on intermediate conclusions, and altered graph connectivity; many of these signatures persist under controls for research field, manuscript length, or graph size. Finally, in a separate detection experiment, ProofTrace distinguishes human-written papers from those disclosing substantive AI use. Its performance remains stable under AI rewording, whereas the performance of state-of-the-art text-based detectors declines sharply.

## 4. Signals, Evidence, and Context: User Scrutiny in Real-World LLM Conversations

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Ahana Biswas, Yu-Ru Lin
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08232v1

**Abstract**

Large language models (LLMs) often present synthesized answers without making their supporting evidence easy to inspect. We study how features of these answers relate to users' subsequent accepting, checking, and challenging in 489,283 assistant-user response pairs from ShareChat and WildChat. Broad attribution language appears in 31.9% of qualifying responses, compared with explicit URLs in 1.33%, revealing a gap between claims of grounding and preserved inspectable support. Attribution, uncertainty, and other response cues generally accompany both checking and challenging; only uncertainty shows a small statistically distinguishable pooled association favoring checking. Within Technical Help, uncertainty favors checking relative to challenging; refusals and reasoning traces favor challenging in Seeking Information. Scrutiny is concentrated in some conversations, while its apparent turn-to-turn persistence becomes much smaller when conversation-level differences and response ordering are examined separately. These results suggest that supporting verification requires attention to what evidence can be inspected, which question a user is trying to resolve, and the history of the interaction.

## 5. Series-wise Cross-validation for Global Time Series Forecasting

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Vitor Cerqueira, Mariana Oliveira, Luis Roque
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08252v1

**Abstract**

We consider the problem of cross-validation (CV) in time series forecasting, with a focus on global forecasting models based on deep learning. The prevailing CV strategy in forecasting is to partition data across time with the last part of each series used for testing. With global models trained on collections of time series, however, a fundamentally different approach becomes possible: partitioning across series and time. We formalize series-wise CV for global forecasting models and benchmark several instantiations, including K-fold CV, bootstrapping, and Monte Carlo CV, on 11 datasets with 10 neural network architectures. We evaluate CV procedures along five dimensions: performance estimation, overoptimism rate, model selection accuracy, regret, and downstream forecasting performance. We withhold a random fraction of series from training so that they appear only in the test set. This protocol simulates the emergence of new series not seen during training, a common scenario in global forecasting. Under this protocol, our results reveal a nuanced trade-off: series-wise CV yields better downstream forecasting accuracy by guiding model selection toward configurations that generalize across both time and series, while time-wise CV retains a slight advantage for estimating out-of-sample error.

## 6. ReproBridge: Building Reproducibility into ML Research Artifacts

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Yubo Wang, Rui Meng, Jaehyun Nam, Tomas Pfister, Jinsung Yoon
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08452v1

**Abstract**

Reproducing machine learning experiments requires reconstructing procedures from information scattered across papers, released code, and supporting artifacts. For coding agents, reproduction success can therefore depend on their ability to fill in missing details, rather than on the released artifacts alone. We ask whether preserving recovered procedures and decisions improves subsequent independent reproduction. We define a standardized artifact design comprising a refined codebase, environment setup, experiment launchers, and a reproduction guide, and introduce ReproBridge, an agent workflow for constructing these refined reproducible artifacts. ReproBridge-Bench provides 1,029 tasks from 387 experiments across 36 papers, anchored by five human-verified reference scores per task. Agents attempt tasks in fresh sessions with raw code or refined artifacts; a separate Auditor checks execution evidence. Across four evaluated models, refined artifacts raise mean reproduction success from 76.4% to 92.6% and reduce downstream API spending by 19.7% on average. The cross-model success-rate spread narrows from 21.6 to 3.9 percentage points, with all four models achieving mean success rates above 90%. Removing any component reduces success in the ablation study, with the largest losses from code and environment removal. These results suggest that preserving execution knowledge helps even models with lower raw-code success reproduce experiments more reliably and at lower API cost.

## 7. ProQuant: Progressive Quantization-aware Training for Edge MLLMs

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Yufei Xue, Yushi Huang, Jiawei Shao, Pingcheng Dong, Yonghao Tan, Shiyao Li, Kwang-Ting Cheng, Jun Zhang
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08216v1

**Abstract**

Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) with impressive perception and understanding capabilities will enable a range of downstream applications. However, their substantial computational and memory requirements hinder the real-world implementation, particularly for resource-constrained edge devices. Quantization has emerged as an effective solution for deploying large models. While post-training quantization (PTQ) successfully retains the performance at moderate bit-width (e.g., W8A8), it suffers from severe performance degradation under low-bit quantization (e.g., W4A8), especially for small-scale models that are more suitable for edge deployment. In this paper, we propose ProQuant, a novel weight-activation quantization-aware training (QAT) framework tailored for low-bit MLLMs. First, to align the limited finetuning dataset distribution with the pretrained dataset distribution, we propose a self-relabeling (SRL) scheme to resample the responses of the open-source multimodal dataset. Second, we are the first to take a decoupled view of multi-modal (MM) understanding and language modeling abilities. We propose a progressive block activation (PBA) mechanism to prioritize the MM understanding recovery. We also introduce a vision-sensitive factor $\vecτ$ that effectively identifies the MM-critical layers. Extensive experiments across various benchmarks, covering models with 2B-8B parameters, show that ProQuant significantly outperforms existing methods. For example, our W4A8 Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct improves average accuracy by impressively 6.6% and achieves performance comparable to full precision counterparts with only approximately 1% performance drop. Code is available at https://github.com/yufeixue-ai/ProQuant/.

## 8. OraRefer: Segmentation-Guided Multi-Photograph Fusion for Oral Cancer Screening with Guaranteed Patient-Level Sensitivity

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Rupam Mukherjee, Mehakdeep Singh, Shirin Dasgupta, Subhamoy Mandal
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08360v1

**Abstract**

Oral cancer and the oral potentially malignant disorders (OPMD) that can precede it are visible on the oral mucosa, which makes smartphone photographs a promising tool for screening. Deep learning models for this task classify individual photographs. However, screening refers patients, not photographs: a patient is usually photographed several times, and the referral decision must miss no more than a set share of patients with disease. We propose OraRefer, a patient-level screening model that reads each photograph with a segmentation-guided foundation model that conditions its class decision on the predicted lesion, combines all photographs of a patient with risk factors, and refers through a threshold with a finite-sample sensitivity guarantee. The model localizes the mouth itself, so inference needs no annotation. On 3000 photographs of 714 patients, patient-level fusion reaches an AUROC of 0.919 and outperforms attention-based multiple-instance learning. At a 90% sensitivity target, OraRefer detects 95% of patients with OPMD or cancer and misses 3 of 61 patients with cancer, at a specificity of 0.610. Because the threshold is calibrated without retraining, each clinic can set its own guaranteed operating point on a local calibration sample.

## 9. On the limitations of molecular representations

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Acer Blake, Fergus Imrie, Charlotte M. Deane
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08420v1

**Abstract**

Molecular representations such as fingerprints and learned embeddings play a central role in cheminformatics and small-molecule machine learning. They serve as the primary input for a diverse array of tasks, including clustering, dataset splitting, and virtual screening, yet their evaluation is often limited to downstream model performance on narrow benchmark tasks. We carried out a systematic investigation of the quality of molecular representations, finding that none of the state-of-the-art molecular embedding models capture graph structure, 3D shape, or ADMET endpoint similarity better than fingerprints or physicochemical descriptors. ## 10. NPCBench: A Clinical Apprenticeship Benchmark for Multimodal Care-Pathway Reasoning in Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma

- **Authors:** Pengkai Wang, Wei-Wei Zhang, Yan Li, Min Tang, Zhitian Hou, Zeyu Liu, Guanghao Zhu, Yuanyi Wang, Yanggan Gu, Wenjun Wang, Minheng Ni, Congkai Xie, Zhijie Sang, Jianmin Wu, Ying Sun, Hongxia Yang
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08369v1

**Abstract**

Current medical benchmarks typically break down clinical competence into isolated factual questions, single-image tasks, or static case vignettes. These tests score individual answers, but reveal less about whether a model can integrate subspecialty knowledge with multimodal evidence and track a patient's condition throughout a course of care. To close this gap, we introduce NPCBench, a clinical apprenticeship benchmark for nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC). NPCBench mirrors specialist training, from guideline and foundational knowledge to case planning, longitudinal review, CarePath integration, and open-ended consultation. It contains 4,777 component and consultation items and a 100-patient CarePath cohort with 417 scored diagnostic and care tasks. ## 11. LLM-Assisted Label Noise Correction for EEG-Based Sleep Stage Classification

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Elmira Salari, Shruti Kshirsagar
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08311v1

**Abstract**

Automatic sleep stage classification from electroencephalography (EEG) signals is a critical task in clinical sleep medicine. While deep learning models have shown promising results in this domain, their performance is fundamentally constrained by the quality of training labels, which human experts manually annotate and that are subject to inter-rater disagreement and systematic labeling errors. In this paper, we propose a novel LLM-assisted label-noise filtering pipeline that operates exclusively during training to identify and remove unreliable sleep-stage annotations before retraining the classifier. Specifically, TinySleepNet is first trained on the original dataset to extract per-epoch SoftMax confidence scores, then low-confidence epochs are submitted to LLaMA 3.1 along with their EEG spectral features and surrounding sequence context. The LLM evaluates each epoch against AASM sleep-scoring guidelines and assigns a verdict of RELIABLE or SUSPICIOUS. Suspicious epochs are removed from the training set, and TinySleepNet is retrained on the cleaned data. Evaluated on the Sleep-EDF Expanded dataset using 20-fold cross-validation, our method improves overall accuracy from 81.8% to 84.6% and Macro F1-score from 75.6% to 77.4%, with particularly notable gains in REM detection. Crucially, because the LLM operates only during training, the lightweight TinySleepNet architecture (1.3M parameters) is preserved at inference, making the proposed pipeline suitable for real-time, wearable sleep monitoring applications.

## 12. Hybrid Machine Learning for Imbalanced Landing-Phase Safety Event Prediction from Flight Data

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Taha Asghar, Bilal Mufti, Syed Qasim Zaheer, Luqman Ahmed
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08386v1

**Abstract**

Landing-phase safety-event prediction requires the detection of rare abnormal approaches within strongly imbalanced multivariate flight data. Capturing coupled temporal behavior while distinguishing event-specific signatures across airport operating contexts remains a central modeling challenge. This study proposes an interpretable hybrid feature-fusion framework for multiclass landing-phase safety-event prediction. A long short-term memory (LSTM) autoencoder trained on nominal flight windows characterizes deviations from routine approach behavior. Its continuous reconstruction-error descriptor is fused with parameter-specific statistical flight-history features and supplied to a class-weighted XGBoost classifier to distinguish Normal, Speed High, Path High, and Flaps Late windows. The framework is evaluated using 53,750 NASA DASHlink approach windows from three arrival airports. Pooled and airport-specific learning strategies are compared alongside component-level benchmarks, repeated pipeline retraining, and feature-attribution analysis. The results demonstrate strong class-balanced detection, with mean macro recall above 0.90 across all airports under repeated partitioning and retraining. Component comparisons establish the importance of class weighting and show that continuous feature fusion outperforms reconstruction-error-only and hard-gate alternatives. Airport-level comparisons reveal benefits from local specialization at the two airports with larger datasets and from pooled training at the airport with the smallest dataset. Feature attributions associate predictions with operationally relevant vertical-path, configuration, speed, and approach-context variables. The framework provides an interpretable approach to rare-event detection and practical guidance for adapting flight-data monitoring models to airport-level data availability.

## 15. FedReason: Large Language Models as Weight-Space Meta-Aggregators for Heterogeneous Federated Learning

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Ali Mohammadi Ruzbahani, Abbas Yazdinejad, Hadis Karimipour
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08383v1

**Abstract**

Federated learning servers combine client models with a fixed aggregation rule, and each rule in common use is designed for one kind of heterogeneity. We study whether a large language model (LLM) that inspects the client models can choose the rule itself, without any training on the server. In FedReason, the LLM is called every ten rounds. It analyses the weights by running code in a restricted server-side sandbox, sees only the printed results, may score a few candidates on a 500-image proxy set, and submits a short Python aggregation program. The server applies the program until the next call and falls back to FedAvg whenever its output is invalid. Full weight tensors never leave the server. We evaluated FedReason with Claude Opus 5.5 on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 under Dirichlet label skew, width-heterogeneous sub-models and Byzantine clients, in 168 runs against 14 baselines. Across 12 paired label-skew runs it beat FedAvg by 1.26 percentage points on average (10 wins), and a control that spends the same per-round proxy budget on choosing among standard rules by 1.08 points. It was the strongest server-side rule on CIFAR-100 but did not beat SCAFFOLD, which changes client training. With three of ten clients malicious, it excluded all sign-flipping clients from the first round and stayed about one point below FLTrust without training a root model. Without proxy feedback, accuracy fell from 73.8% to 38.8% in one seed. A 60-round run cost 0.79 USD in API fees on average.

- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05

**Abstract**

## 18. DictLLM: Post-training Compression of Large Language Model with Dictionary Kernels

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Jinho Park, Se Young Chun, Mingoo Seok
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08082v1

**Abstract**

Efficient deployment of large language models (LLMs) on mobile devices requires balancing performance with compute and memory constraints. Post-training compression is an effective approach to managing this trade-off, while requiring much less computation than in-training counterparts. We propose DictLLM, a dictionary-based compression scheme in which weight matrices within grouped layers are decomposed into a shared dictionary and layer-specific sparse coefficients, directly obtained from a pre-trained model without further training. Specifically, we cast DictLLM as a four-step post-training optimization. First, we obtain shared dictionaries via singular value decomposition (SVD) of input-scaled weight matrices, amortizing rank across grouped layers; second, we sparsify the layer-specific coefficients to the target compression ratio using a dictionary-aware Hessian; and third, we refine the dictionaries given the resulting sparse coefficients. Finally, we aggressively quantize both components using factor-specific Hessian information: input covariance for the dictionaries and the dictionary-aware Hessian for the coefficients. The resulting representation reduces memory footprint by storing a single shared dictionary per layer group, along with sparse layer-specific coefficients. On language modeling and zero-shot reasoning benchmarks, DictLLM consistently outperforms existing post-training low-rank compression methods and pushes the Pareto frontier between model size and performance. DictLLM compresses the original LLaMA-7B model by 5.1x with only a 2.9-point increase in WikiText-2 perplexity, achieving a 3.6x higher compression ratio than the state-of-the-art low-rank method.

## 21. Data-driven discovery of intrinsic Lie point symmetries of differential equation systems

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Ilker Gurcan, Kadir Ustun, Ramazan Gokberk Cinbis
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08421v1

**Abstract**

Discovering continuous symmetries directly from differential-equation data can reveal geometric structure for model reduction and scientific machine learning without symbolic determining equations or a closed-form representation of the governing system. We formulate intrinsic Lie point-symmetry discovery as an operator-theoretic problem on sampled jet-space data. Admissible generators are identified from a sampled determining operator and separated from redundant, solution-dependent, and geometrically trivial directions. The framework combines prolongation, sequential subspace restrictions, and a fixed nonlinear dictionary linear in trainable coefficients. Here we show that explicit intrinsic continuous-symmetry generators can be recovered from data obtained from exact or approximate differential systems.

## 22. Conformal Prediction for Time-Dependent PDEs

- **Journal:** arXiv
- **Authors:** Joshua Stiller, Annika Schneider, Eyke Hüllermeier
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08409v1

**Abstract**

Uncertainty quantification is crucial in scientific machine learning, where models inform safety-critical tasks such as flood forecasting, financial risk management, and thermal control in machines. Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees, but in time-dependent settings common to physics and engineering, these guarantees can break down, leading to systematic undercoverage. We study this problem in the context of surrogate models for time-dependent physical systems described by partial differential equations. We prove that in a function space setting, distributions at arbitrarily close times can be mutually singular, making exact coverage guarantees impossible. We further show that in discretized settings, the total variation distance between distributions often saturates exponentially fast with the grid resolution, mandating restrictions on the spatial discretization to ensure coverage. Finally, for certain settings, we show how to use principled weighted conformal prediction to obtain finite-sample coverage guarantees over growing time horizons.

Although further research is needed, this study shows the initial feasibility of conversational AI in a real-world setting-assessed via conversation safety and quality, as well as user acceptance-and represents a crucial step towards clinical translation.
